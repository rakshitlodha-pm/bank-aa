Merchant match list

The brands engineering matches on, the strings they arrive as, and what each one lets us say. Where the brand is listed we name the company. Where it isn't, we fall back to the sector and name the listed players in it.

How to read this

  • Tier 1 — the brand is itself listed in India. Direct nudge: “you spend at Blinkit → Blinkit is Eternal”. Strongest card we have.
  • Tier 2 — private Indian brand, sector has listed players. Sector nudge: “you spend on quick commerce → Eternal, Swiggy, DMart”.
  • Tier 3 — no nudge. Either foreign-listed and unbuyable here (Nike, MakeMyTrip, Netflix), a payment gateway that must be suppressed entirely (Razorpay, PayU), or a sector with no listed player (mental health, donations, software subscriptions). The insight still shows — only the buy button is withheld.
  • Match strings are what the trie is built on: merchantName variants plus VPA handle prefixes. Match on the VPA first — it is far more stable than the display name.
  • “dump” means the alias strings were harvested from the evidence pull and are real. “add” means the brand was added by name and its aliases are expected forms that must be confirmed against your own narration corpus.
  • “verify” flags a listing status I am not confident enough about to ship — recent IPOs and acquisitions. Equity desk confirms before these go live.

What this list does not solve

  
    
    
  • Payment gateways. Razorpay was ₹8,90,000 — 20.4% of outflow in the
      evidence dump and resolves to nothing useful. It must be tagged
      aggregator_opaque and excluded from every merchant surface, not shown as a top
      merchant.
    • 
    
  • One brand, many strings. Zomato and District arrive as 7 distinct
      merchantName values totalling ₹34,426. Match only the largest and you tell
      the user ₹8,671 — a 4× understatement on the card the mechanic depends on.
      Every row here is a brand with its alias set, which is why 149 brands is more like
      600 strings.
    • 
    
  • Truncation. 85 of 160 merchant names in the dump arrive clipped to ≤10 characters
      — 1MG HEALTH, 10112APOLL, AIRBNB PAY,
      NIKE BHANN. Prefix matching is mandatory; short prefixes will collide and need a
      tie-break on sector.
    • 
    
  • Foreign brands. Recognisable, high-value, unbuyable here — 13.8% of commercial
      spend in the dump. Sector fallback doesn't rescue them either: Nike → Bata is a
      stretch a user would find silly. Insight yes, nudge no.
    • 
    
  • Individuals. 158 distinct people, 17.4% of outflow. Never a merchant. Rent is the one
      exception worth extracting, and it comes from behaviour — a stable payee paid on the
      1st–7th, preceded by a deposit at 2–3× the monthly amount.

Coverage basis. Alias strings marked “dump” are harvested from a single
    consented AA pull — 3 accounts, 1,265 transactions, Aug 2025–Sep 2026 — in which
    the top 100 match keys carried 97.2% of matched value and the top 50 carried 91.9%. That
    is the evidence a ~150-brand list clears a 90% accuracy bar; it is one user, so the head must be
    re-mined from your own corpus before the list is frozen.

  
Accuracy means share of transaction value resolved to a brand — not share of rows.
    The local long tail and payments to individuals are explicitly out of scope; engineering should
    not chase them.
