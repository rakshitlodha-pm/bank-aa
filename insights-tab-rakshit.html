1

One of the four numbers is negative

Left over is −₹40,558. It is not a bug. Your ₹1,26,820 card bill and your ₹1,45,000 mandate both debited on 1 August, and salary did not land until the 31st. Money in minus spent minus invested is genuinely negative for the month.

Two things to decide. Is “Left over” the right word when it goes below zero — and does a negative month need its own frame, or does it just sit there in clay and let the user draw the conclusion? I have left it plain on purpose.

2

The obvious comparison would have lied

Spent is compared to your six-month usual of ₹1,84,074, not to last month. Last month was July, when you spent ₹4,78,942. Against July, August reads as down 35% and looks like restraint. Against your own median it is 1.7× hot, which is the truth.

One bonus month poisons every month-on-month comparison for a year. The engine should compare to a median by default and only offer month-on-month as a second view.

3

Your July destroys the chart

₹15,02,386 came in that month. It is 4.6× your usual, so it pins the scale and squashes the other five months into stubs.

I kept it. Clipping the bar or dropping the month would hide the most consequential event in your year. Instead there is a dashed line at your usual month so the other bars still mean something, and July gets a sentence underneath. The alternative — a separate “unusual month” treatment that lifts outliers out of the series — is worth a look, but it is more machinery than one chart deserves.

4

Current balance, as you asked — plus one free fact

Three accounts, ₹13,55,326, and 96% of it in one ICICI account. Federal Bank holds ₹39.

The dump also tells us Federal’s branch field reads “Indmoney” and the account opened on 26 June 2025. So we know that account exists to fund an investing app, without matching a single merchant string. Account metadata is a cheaper signal than transactions and we are not using it yet.

5

Every buy button is below the fold

The dashed red line is roughly where a phone screen ends on first paint. Above it: four numbers, a chart, three balances. Nothing to act on. Section 2 — the entire commercial payload — starts below.

If the tab is measured on engagement and retention that may be fine; the numbers are the reason people open it. If it is also expected to convert, one insight has to be hoisted above the fold, and the honest candidate is the idle-balance one, because it needs no merchant data and fires for everyone.

6

Your biggest category is the one we know nothing about

Credit card bills are ₹1,26,820 — 40.6% of August. Treating the card as an expense is right for the totals, and it is what you decided. But it also means the largest bar in the chart has no merchant, no sector and no stock behind it.

Whatever you bought on that card in July is invisible to us. This is not fixable inside the Insights tab; it needs card-statement consent, which is a separate AA data type. Worth knowing that the ceiling on merchant coverage for a heavy card user is set here, not by the merchant list.

7

Both of your structures fired in the same month

Blinkit is the merchant-level one: ₹15,054 in August, 91 orders and ₹1,21,600 across the year, and its parent Eternal is listed. The insight names the brand you actually use.

Travel is the category-level one, exactly as you described it: ₹20,998 in August, all MakeMyTrip, which is not listed in India — so it climbs to the sector and offers Ixigo, EaseMyTrip and IRCTC. Your Ixigo example turned out to be real in your own data.

8

The two ratio tiles do not add to 100

74.9% spent and 34.8% invested sum to 109.7%, which is the same fact as the negative Left over, said in percentages. I would rather show two honest ratios that overflow than force them into a split that hides the overspend.

9

Eight insights evaluated, seven fired, one held

Three P0s, three P1s, one P2 — and health held back by rule. I have shown all of them stacked so you can read the ranking; in the app I would render three and put the rest behind an expander, because eight cards is a scroll nobody finishes.

The held one matters more than the seven that fired. Health was your third-largest category at ₹35,934, ₹31,270 of it mental-health care, and it has a perfectly good listed nudge in Apollo and Global Health. It does not get one. That rule costs us a slot on a user whose data is otherwise rich, and it should still hold.

10

What we deliberately do not say

₹1,45,000 leaves every month against a narration that reads only ACH/RAZORPAY PAYMENTS PV, same mandate reference each time. Your ET Money mandate stopped in January 2026 and this one started in February at the identical ₹1,45,000. It is almost certainly the same money under a new rail.

We do not say so. Almost certainly is not a thing to tell a user about their money, and this is the same class of mistake as reading your Paytm salary as an investment. So the biggest single line in your outflow gets counted as investment and left unnamed. Another ₹35,650 sits in person-to-person transfers we also cannot name.

11

A rich user, two days in, is a thin-data user

This is the same person with thirteen months of history and 24 matched brands in August. On 2 September he has nine transactions, two categories and zero matched merchants.

The period selector manufactures the cold-start problem for everybody. Whatever fallbacks we build for a Tier 2 user with sparse data are the same fallbacks that keep this page alive on the 2nd of every month — which means they are not an edge case we can defer, they are load-bearing for every user on roughly a third of the days in a month.

12

The rule that keeps Section 2 from going blank

Eligibility looks back twelve months; the figure shown respects the period. So the Blinkit card still appears on 2 September, and it says ₹1,21,600 over the year rather than ₹0 this month.

Without that split you get an empty section. With it, the merchant insight degrades to a yearly statement instead of vanishing. It is one rule and it does most of the work of the entire fallback story.

13

A partial month cannot have a Left over

Income is lumpy and arrives late — your salary lands after the 24th. So on the 2nd, Money in is ₹0 and Left over would read −₹1,95,855, which is arithmetically true and completely useless.

So it is withheld and labelled, and Spent is compared to the same two days of August — ₹50,855 against ₹1,70,259. Compared to the whole of August instead, the same two days would read as down 84%, which would be a compliment the user has not earned yet.

14

Section 3 is the only part that does not move

Both frames show the same cohort card, because a percentile against other investors has nothing to do with which month you are looking at. That makes it the most reliable section on the page and the cheapest to build — it needs no AA consent at all, just Paytm Money’s own investing data.

Your growth-loop idea sits here and it is the strongest thing in this whole conversation: the comparison is the reason to connect a bank account. Lead with “see where you stand” on the consent screen rather than “see your expenses,” and Section 3 pays for its own cohort.

The slot inventory

There is no persona branch anywhere in it — every user gets the same thirteen candidates evaluated in the same order, and the page is whatever survives the thresholds. What is real, and what I made up

Computed from your dump

  • Every rupee figure, date, brand, balance and ratio on both phones
  • 24 brands resolved in August from 51 merchant transactions
  • The SBI EMI, identified from RACPC II GURUGRAM at ₹6,360 across 13 months with zero variation — your bank had tagged it as income tax
  • Rent, found from a person-to-person transfer, not a merchant
  • The May and June mandate gap

Assumed — three numbers

  • Top 3% of Paytm Money investors
  • Median monthly investment of ₹18,400 in the ₹3–5L income band
  • 1,340 users have connected a bank, against a threshold of 5,000

Nothing else on either page reads from these. Delete Section 3 entirely and the rest still renders.

Not modelled at all

  • Live prices. So there is not a single performance figure anywhere — no “you would have made X.” Your asymmetric rule plugs in exactly where the buy and view buttons already sit
  • Your holdings. The deposits dump has none, so the engine cannot yet say “you already own this” — which it must, before any buy button ships
  • External mutual funds via CAS

The ceiling, stated plainly

  • ₹1,26,820 — a card bill we cannot see inside
  • ₹1,45,000 — a monthly mandate behind a payment gateway
  • ₹35,650 — transfers to people
  • Together 67% of your August outflow is money we can count but not name. The merchant list works on the third that is left, and on that third it is close to complete.

Three things I could not decide for you. Whether Left over should stay negative or get its own treatment. Whether one insight gets hoisted above the fold, and if so whether it is the idle balance. And whether Section 2 shows three cards with an expander or all seven stacked as they are here. Everything else in the design follows from decisions you have already made.

Source. A single Account Aggregator deposits dump — 1,265 transactions across three savings accounts, 2 August 2025 to 1 September 2026. Personal identifiers in the file (PAN, date of birth, address, email, mobile) were read and excluded from everything here.

Caution. One user is one user. Coverage rates that hold for a Gurgaon salary account with a ₹1.45L monthly mandate are not the rates you will see across Paytm Money’s base, and the thresholds in the slot inventory are first guesses that need tuning against real traffic.
